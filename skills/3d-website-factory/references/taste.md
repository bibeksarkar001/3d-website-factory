# Taste

## The slop list: tells that a site was generated
Design:
- Indigo-to-violet or purple-to-pink gradients, especially on the hero or the main button.
- Three identical feature cards in a row, each an icon, a two-word title and a sentence.
- Frosted glass panels floating over a gradient blob.
- The same large corner radius on everything.
- One generic sans-serif for everything at default weights. No typographic contrast.
- Gradient text on the headline. Glowing borders. Emoji as icons.
- A centred hero with a badge pill above the headline ("✨ New: ..."), or a tiny uppercase letter-spaced
  eyebrow label sitting right above an oversized headline. Fold the kicker into the headline or the nav instead.
- Every section the same height, same layout, same rhythm.
- Stock 3D blobs and abstract shapes that mean nothing.
- Logos of companies that are not customers.
- A warm cream or beige page by reflex, or near-black by reflex. The background must come from the brand and the hero.
- The overused faces: Inter, Roboto, Geist, Plus Jakarta Sans, Space Grotesk, Fraunces, Instrument Sans,
  Poppins, Montserrat. Fine when the brand already uses them; never as the default pick.

Words:
- "Unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "revolutionize", "supercharge", "empower", "harness", "game-changer",
  "in today's fast-paced world", "take it to the next level", "effortless", "cutting-edge", "robust".
- Triplets ("Fast. Simple. Powerful."). Questions as headlines ("Ready to transform your workflow?").
- Headlines about the product instead of the visitor's life.
- Vague claims without a fact behind them ("trusted by thousands").
- Em dashes everywhere.

## Copy that works
- **Don't make me think.** The visitor gets it on the first read. Short words, short sentences, no maths.
- **Name the pain first,** in the visitor's words, then the relief.
- **Be specific.** "Five minutes on the train counts" beats "Learn anywhere".
- **One screen, one point, one ask.**
- **Buttons say what happens next.** "Start your first lesson", not "Get started".
- After editing, show the user the old line and the new line for every change, so they own the result.

## Choosing fonts
Pick for the brand's character, then check the licence covers commercial use.
- Free and safe: Google Fonts (all open licences), Fontshare (free for commercial use), the system font stack.
- Less common, good free faces to consider: Fontshare's Satoshi, General Sans, Clash Display, Cabinet Grotesk,
  Erode, Sentient, Zodiak; Google's Bricolage Grotesque, Familjen Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk, Newsreader, Young Serif,
  Gloock, Syne, Unbounded, Instrument Serif (display only). Rotate; do not settle on one favourite.
- See real use first: fontsinuse.com, typewolf.com.
- Pairing: one display face with character for headlines, one plain face for body. Or one family with a
  real range of weights and widths.
- Display sizes are big and tight (letter spacing -1% to -3%). Body is 16 to 19 px, line height about 1.5.
- Load only the weights you use, with `font-display: swap`.

## Moves that read as expensive (options, not rules)
- A two-tone headline: first line in the text colour, second in the muted colour.
- An elegant serif display with small, tracked detail lines in a mono or grotesk.
- Huge type set behind the 3D object, the object overlapping a letter.
- One accent colour used in one place per screen.
